## Science Goal

Coulomb excitation measurement of unstable **³⁴ᵐCl** (isomeric state, Eₓ = 146 keV, t½ ≈ 32 min) to determine:
- **B(E2; 3⁺₁ → 1⁺₁)** and **B(E2; 3⁺₁ → 1⁺₂)** transition strengths
- These define the **thermal equilibrium temperature** between ³⁴gCl and ³⁴ᵐCl in classical nova explosions
- Also: Coulomb excitation of **³⁴gCl** to study the anomalously high B(E2; 0⁺₁ → 2⁺₁, T=1) — possible isospin mixing

## Why It Matters

- ³⁴Cl isomers produced in novae via ³³S(p,γ) and β-decay of ³⁴Ar
- If ground/isomeric states are **not** in thermal equilibrium: treated as separate nuclei in reaction networks → large effect on final ³⁴S abundance (presolar grain signature of nova origin)
- If equilibrium temperature is lower than predicted: ³⁴ᵐCl cosmic γ-rays may be observable (future COSI mission)
- Current transition strengths based only on Weisskopf estimates (factor ~10 uncertainty)

## Key Physics

- Observe **461-keV M1 γ-ray** (1⁺, 461-keV → ³⁴Cl ground state) to extract B(E2) for the unobserved 315-keV transition (1⁺, 461-keV → 3⁺ isomer)
- The 315-keV transition rate governs thermal equilibrium onset at T ≈ 0.2 GK in novae
